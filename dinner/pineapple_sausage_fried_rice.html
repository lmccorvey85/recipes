Golden Pineapple Butter-Glazed Sausage Fried Rice (Andouille + Italian Sausage)

Overview

Pineapple fried rice built on seared mild Italian sausage and smoked andouille instead of chicken, finished with a butter glaze. Add at least one vegetable side when serving as a full dinner — good pairings are doctored green beans, asparagus, Brussels sprouts, or roasted broccoli. Avoid honey-butter carrots since carrots are already in the rice. The vegetable side is not baked into the main dish itself.

Ingredients

Sausage & Rice

  • 1 lb mild Italian sausage, casings removed, pinched into ¾" pieces
  • 12–14 oz smoked andouille sausage, sliced into ¼" half-moons
  • 6 cups cold cooked rice (day-old, or Success Rice cooked ahead and chilled — 3 regular bags or 2 family-size bags, check box yield)

Fruit & Veg

  • 20 oz can pineapple chunks, drained and patted dry, ¼ cup juice reserved
  • ⅓ cup golden raisins, soaked 10 min in warm water, drained
  • 3 eggs
  • 1 medium onion, diced
  • Optional: 1 green bell pepper, diced
  • 2–3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 cup frozen peas & carrots
  • 2–3 scallions, sliced

Sauce (double sauce amount)

  • ¼ cup low-sodium soy sauce
  • 2 Tbsp oyster sauce
  • ¼ cup reserved pineapple juice
  • 2 Tbsp water or unsalted broth
  • 1 Tbsp brown sugar
  • 2 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp garlic powder, 1 tsp onion powder
  • ½ tsp ground ginger, ½ tsp black pepper
  • 1 tsp sesame oil
  • Optional, only if flat after tasting: 1 tsp fish sauce OR ¼ tsp Tony Chachere's (not both)

Butter Glaze & Finishing

  • 2–3 Tbsp neutral oil, divided (for wok batches)
  • 1 Tbsp butter + 1 Tbsp sauce, for glazing the seared sausage
  • 1 Tbsp butter, for scrambling the eggs
  • Lime wedges, optional, to serve
Sodium note: sausages are already salty — no bouillon, no added salt. No vinegar finish; lime only, if wanted.

Equipment

  • 14" carbon steel wok (Yosukata)
  • Sheet pan (for chilling rice ahead)
  • Meat thermometer
  • Wok spatula, tongs, mixing bowls

Rice Needed (Success Rice)

  • Cooked rice needed: 6 cups
  • Regular Success Rice bags: 3
  • Family-size bags: 2
  • Prep note: cook unsalted, spread in a thin layer on a sheet pan, chill uncovered 1 hr+ (overnight is better) — or freeze 20 min in a pinch. Cold, dried-out rice is what fries properly instead of steaming into mush. Check box yield, sizes vary.

Ingredient Prep

  1. Remove casings from Italian sausage, pinch into rough ¾" pieces. Slice andouille into ¼" half-moons.
  2. Drain pineapple chunks well, pat completely dry with paper towels (reserve ¼ cup of the juice for the sauce) — a wet pineapple won't sear, it'll steam.
  3. Soak raisins in warm water 10 min, drain.
  4. Dice onion and bell pepper (if using), mince garlic.
  5. Whisk sauce ingredients together in a bowl; set aside.
  6. Crack eggs into a small bowl, beat lightly.
  7. Have cold rice broken up and ready to add — break apart any large clumps with your hands before it hits the wok.

Detailed Cooking Instructions (Wok)

1. Preheat the wok over high heat until it just begins to lightly smoke. This is the single most important step for fried rice texture — a cold or under-heated wok steams instead of frying.

2. Sear the pineapple first, dry, no oil (2–3 min), tossing occasionally until edges caramelize and turn golden. Remove to a bowl.

3. Sear the sausages in batches. Add 1 Tbsp oil. Add Italian sausage pieces in a single layer — don't crowd, work in 2 batches if needed. Sear 4–5 min, turning, until browned on multiple sides and internal temp hits 160°F. Remove to a bowl. Add andouille in a single layer, sear 2–3 min until just browned on the cut sides (it's pre-cooked, so you're building color/fond, not cooking it through). Remove to the same bowl. Toss the warm sausages with 1 Tbsp butter + 1 Tbsp of the prepared sauce — this is the butter glaze. Set aside.

4. Scramble the eggs. Add 1 Tbsp butter to the wok over medium heat. Pour in beaten eggs, soft-scramble just until set (don't overcook — they'll cook a bit more once folded back in later). Remove to the bowl with the sausage.

5. Build the aromatics. Add remaining oil if needed, back to high heat. Add onion, bell pepper (if using), and garlic. Stir fry 1–2 min until just softened and fragrant — don't let the garlic burn. Add frozen peas & carrots, toss 1 min.

6. Fry the rice in two additions. Push veg to one side, add half the cold rice to the open space, press and toss over high heat 1–2 min to break up clumps and heat through. Add the second half, repeat. Keep everything moving — this is where "wok hei" (that light charred fragrance) develops.

7. Sauce it. Build to about 75% of the sauce first, drizzling around the edges of the wok and tossing to coat evenly. Taste. Add more sauce gradually until it's right — remember the sausages are salty, so under-salt rather than over. Add fish sauce or Tony's only now, and only if it tastes flat.

8. Fold everything back in. Return seared sausages (with their butter glaze), seared pineapple, scrambled eggs, drained raisins, and sliced scallions to the wok. Toss 1–2 min over high heat just until everything is hot and coated. Finish with a squeeze of lime if using.

Visual Doneness Cues

  • Pineapple: golden caramelized edges, not translucent/raw-looking.
  • Italian sausage: browned on multiple sides, 160°F internal.
  • Andouille: just browned on cut faces — it's pre-cooked, you're building fond/color only.
  • Eggs: soft-set curds, not dry or rubbery.
  • Rice: individual grains separate and coated, not clumpy or mushy; slight char/fragrance at the edges.

Keeping Warm

Fried rice is best served immediately off the wok. If it must hold, keep uncovered or loosely covered over the lowest possible heat for up to 10 min — covering tightly traps steam and turns the rice soft.

Storage

Cool completely, store in an airtight container. Fridge up to 4 days. Freezes well up to 2 months — portion before freezing for easier reheating.

Reheating

Covered skillet or wok over medium heat with a small splash of water, stirring occasionally, until heated through. Microwave option: covered with a damp paper towel, stirring halfway. Avoid high, dry heat — it dries the rice out further.
